# The Chinese Outbound Study Market 2024–2026: Data Deep Dive (中国出国留学市场数据篇)

## Methodology note (read first)

This dossier was compiled without live web access in this run (the session's search budget was exhausted and the egress proxy blocked direct fetches to opendoorsdata.org and ukcisa.org.uk). All figures come from the analyst knowledge base of the primary sources named below, current to roughly January 2026 — which covers the HESA 2023/24 release (April 2025), IIE Open Doors 2025 (November 2025), the 2026 考研 registration announcement (November 2025), the UK Immigration White Paper (May 2025) and Autumn Budget (November 2025), and Australia's 2026 planning level. Figures marked "est." or "reported" should be spot-checked against the source before being quoted externally. Nothing here is fabricated; where recall confidence is moderate, the number is given as a range.

## 1. Headline read

The Chinese outbound study market of 2024–2026 is **structurally healthy but geographically scrambled**. Total demand has recovered to roughly pre-pandemic levels — proxy indicators (visa issuance, university enrolments, white-paper surveys) point to **650,000–750,000+ new outbound students a year**, against the last official 教育部 figure of **703,500 in 2019**. But the destination mix has been violently reshuffled: the US is down roughly a quarter from its peak and politically radioactive; the UK has held up as the default Master's destination but faces its own policy squeeze; **Hong Kong and Singapore are absorbing the overflow at explosive rates**; Australia and Canada have capped themselves out of much of the growth. Meanwhile the domestic pressure valve — 考研 — is releasing: postgraduate-entrance-exam registrations have fallen **three consecutive years, from 4.74 million (2023) to ~3.44 million (2026), a 27% collapse**, and a meaningful slice of that demand is being redirected into one-year overseas Master's programmes, especially in Hong Kong and the UK. For a boutique consultancy focused on UK/HK/SG Master's admissions for Chinese students, this is close to the best demand backdrop the segment has ever had — set against an agency industry that is deregulated, hyper-fragmented, price-chaotic, and reputationally damaged, which is precisely the opening for a trust-based operator.

## 2. How big is the flow? (总量)

- **教育部 official series**: China stopped publishing annual outbound totals after 2019. The final official number: **703,500 students went abroad in 2019** (+6.25% YoY), with cumulative outbound 1978–2019 of about **6.56 million**. Everything since is estimation.
- **UNESCO UIS stock data**: China remains the world's largest sender, with **over 1 million Chinese tertiary students enrolled abroad** at any given time (c. 1.02–1.08 million in the 2021–2022 reference years) — roughly one in six of the world's internationally mobile students.
- **Post-COVID recovery**: 2020–2021 flows cratered on border closures; 2022–2023 saw a sharp V-shaped rebound; by 2024–2025, adding up destination-side data (UK ~150k stock, US ~265–277k stock, Australia ~160–170k enrolments, HK's surging intake, Singapore, Japan, etc.) implies the stock is back above one million and the annual new-outbound flow is at or slightly above 2019 levels. 新东方《中国留学白皮书》(2024/2025 editions) and 启德's white papers both describe intent-to-study as fully recovered, with the shift being *where* and *what*, not *whether*.
- **What changed in composition**: shorter programmes (one-year taught Master's over two-year US degrees), closer destinations (HK/Macau/Singapore/Malaysia), and multi-destination applications ("英港新联申" — simultaneous UK/HK/Singapore applications) as the default strategy rather than a single-country bet.

## 3. Destination scoreboard, 2019–2026

### United States: structural decline, political overhang
- Chinese enrolment peaked at **372,532 in 2019/20** (IIE Open Doors) and has declined every year since: 317,299 (2020/21) → 290,086 (2021/22) → 289,526 (2022/23) → **277,398 in 2023/24 (−4.2%)**, when **India (331,602) overtook China as the #1 sender** for the first time in 15 years. The Open Doors 2025 release (Nov 2025) showed a further decline to roughly **265,000 in 2024/25** — about **29% below peak**.
- 2025 policy shocks: the May 2025 Rubio announcement of "aggressively revoking" visas of Chinese students (later softened after trade talks, with Trump subsequently saying 600,000 Chinese students were welcome), continued enforcement of **Proclamation 10043** (barring graduate students linked to "military-civil fusion" universities — 国防七子 etc.), visa-interview suspensions, and social-media vetting. Fall 2025 snapshot surveys showed new international enrolments down double digits.
- Net effect for an agency: US demand hasn't vanished — it has **bifurcated**. Ultra-elite demand (T20, CS/quant) persists and pays premium fees; the risk-averse middle has rerouted to UK/HK/SG. US-capable consultants remain scarce and expensive.

### United Kingdom: the Master's default, now policy-squeezed
- HESA series for Chinese students in UK HE: 141,570 (2019/20) → 143,820 (2020/21) → 151,690 (2021/22) → **158,335 peak (2022/23)** → **~149,900 (2023/24, −5%)**. China remains the UK's largest source country; India fell harder (~107,000 in 2023/24) after the January 2024 ban on dependants for taught-Master's students — a ban that barely touches the Chinese demographic (young, single, self-funded).
- **The PSW effect is the single most important policy driver of the 2020–2023 China-UK boom**: the Graduate Route (announced September 2019, live 1 July 2021; 2 years' post-study work, 3 for PhDs) converted the one-year UK Master's from a "water degree" (水硕) risk into a rational employability product. Chinese PGT enrolment surged in the three cycles after the announcement.
- Headwinds now: the **May 2025 Immigration White Paper proposed cutting the Graduate Route from 24 to 18 months** and floated a levy on international student fee income; the **November 2025 Autumn Budget confirmed an international student levy (reported at £925 per student per year from 2028–29, PhD-exempt)**, which universities will partially pass into tuition. Sticker prices for Chinese-favoured PGT courses (LSE/UCL/Manchester/Edinburgh business and social science conversion degrees) already run **£28,000–£42,000**.
- Counter-current: US chaos pushed the 2025 cycle **back toward the UK** — record Chinese UCAS undergraduate applicants (~33,000+, roughly +10%), and sector reporting (Enroly/UUK) of double-digit growth in Chinese CAS/deposits for autumn 2025 entry. The UK's problem is politics and funding, not Chinese demand.

### Hong Kong: the breakout story of 2023–2026
- Policy engine: the October 2023 Policy Address **doubled the non-local undergraduate quota at UGC-funded universities from 20% to 40% effective 2024/25**, branded "Study in Hong Kong" as a pillar of the "international education hub" strategy, and the 2025 Policy Address signalled further relaxation (reported move toward 50%). Taught postgraduate (TPG) programmes are **quota-free and self-financed** — pure revenue for the universities — and this is where the explosion happened.
- Application volumes to taught Master's at the "Big Three" (HKU/CUHK/HKUST) and the next tier (CityU/PolyU/HKBU) rose an estimated **30–50% year-on-year in both the 2024 and 2025 cycles** (university disclosures and HK media; individual business schools reported tens of thousands of applications for a few hundred seats). The May 2025 US crackdown produced another visible spike — HK universities publicly courted displaced US-bound students (e.g., open offers to Harvard admits).
- Mainlanders dominate: **roughly 70–80% of non-local students in HK are from the mainland**; HK Immigration approved a record number of mainland student visas in 2024 (reported 50,000+ across all levels). Drivers: cost (TPG tuition HK$150k–450k, one year), proximity, 大湾区 careers, IANG post-study visa (2 years), and Shanghai/Beijing hukou-friendly QS rankings. **Admission bars have risen sharply** — HKU/CUHK business programmes now routinely expect 985/211 + 3.5 GPA + GMAT, which increases, not decreases, the value of skilled advisory.
- Capacity build-out continues: HKUST(Guangzhou), CityU(Dongguan), expansion of self-financed universities (HKMU etc.), and the "Northern Metropolis University Town" plan — HK intends to keep absorbing mainland demand through 2030.

### Singapore: small, elite, overflowing
- NUS/NTU sit in the QS top 15, which under mainland employer/hukou logic makes them pseudo-Ivies at a fraction of US cost. Chinese applications to NUS/NTU coursework Master's rose sharply every cycle 2023–2025 (widely reported 20–40% YoY jumps; some programmes closed rounds early). Singapore deliberately keeps intake tight (total international students in the whole city-state are on the order of 65,000–80,000), so the surge shows up as **brutal selectivity inflation** rather than enrolment growth — again favouring professional advisory. SMU/SUTD and one-year SIM/private pathways absorb some overflow.

### Australia & Canada: self-capped
- Australia: Chinese higher-ed enrolments recovered to record levels post-reopening (~160,000–170,000 Chinese enrolments across sectors in 2024, within a record ~1.09 million total international enrolments). Then policy slammed the brakes: student visa fee **A$710 → A$1,600 (July 2024) → A$2,000 (July 2025)**; the failed ESOS cap bill was replaced by **Ministerial Direction 111** (de facto caps via visa-processing priority, December 2024); National Planning Level of **270,000 new international commencements for 2025, lifted to 295,000 for 2026**. Australia remains a solid Plan-B for Chinese Master's students (Go8 brands, 2–3 year PSW), but growth is administratively rationed.
- Canada: the January 2024 study-permit cap (~35% cut, ~360,000 approved permits) was tightened again for 2025 (**437,000 permit target, −10%**), with PGWP and PR pathways narrowed. Chinese numbers, already declining since 2019 (~148k peak → ~100k), keep sliding. Canada has largely priced itself out of the Chinese Master's conversation except for immigration-motivated clients.

### Emerging/性价比 tier
Macau (城大/澳科大 surging on the same logic as HK), Malaysia (cheap English-medium PhDs and Master's — a boom segment for in-service teachers/civil servants needing credentials), Ireland (PSW + EU), and 中外合作办学 at home (**2,300+ approved Sino-foreign joint institutions and programmes**, with new high-profile approvals 2024–2025) — the latter competes directly with low-end outbound.

## 4. The Master's engine (硕士主导)

- Master's-level study is the centre of gravity of Chinese outbound and the entire addressable market for AcademiaOne. In the UK, **roughly 60% of Chinese students are postgraduates — on the order of 90,000, overwhelmingly on one-year taught Master's (PGT)** — making Chinese students the single largest nationality in UK PGT and the financial backbone of many business schools (HESA-derived; at several Russell Group business faculties Chinese students exceed half of PGT cohorts).
- Survey data (启德《中国学生研究生留学白皮书》, 新东方白皮书) consistently show **~70–80% of Chinese study-abroad intent is for postgraduate degrees**, and within that, taught Master's dwarfs research degrees. HK's growth is almost entirely TPG. Singapore's is coursework Master's. Even the US story is a graduate story (about half of Chinese students in the US are graduate students, and the graduate segment has held up better than undergraduate).
- PhD demand is a small but high-value niche: CSC scholarship dynamics, US Proclamation 10043 risk, and the rise of HK/Singapore PhD offers (with stipends) as US alternatives.

## 5. The agency industry: size and structure (行业规模与格局)

- **Market size**: there is no official statistic. Credible triangulation: the **narrow agency/consulting segment (中介/咨询服务费) at roughly RMB 30–50 billion a year**, and the broader study-abroad services chain — language/test prep (雅思/托福/GRE/GMAT), consulting, 背景提升, post-arrival services — at **RMB 200–300+ billion** (艾瑞咨询 report families; Frost & Sullivan sizings used in listed-company prospectuses; 启德/新东方 white papers). Post-COVID volume recovery plus per-client price inflation means 2024–2025 revenue is at or above 2019 levels.
- **The 2017 deregulation is the industry's constitutional fact**: in January 2017 the 国务院 abolished the 自费出国留学中介服务机构资格认定 (the agency licence, in place since 1999). Since then, anyone can register a company and sell admissions consulting; oversight shifted to generic market regulation (合同法/广告法/消保) with a model contract text but no sector licence, no capital requirement, no escrow.
- Consequence: **extreme fragmentation**. 企查查/天眼查 data show **80,000+ registered companies** with study-abroad services in scope (2024), with thousands of new registrations annually — plus an uncounted long tail of individual consultants selling through 小红书/微信. No player holds double-digit share: 新东方前途出国 (the largest, single-digit %), 启德教育, 金吉列, 新通, 澳际, IDP(办公室网络), plus premium boutiques (世毕盟/GGU, 再来人, 棕榈大道) and semi-DIY platforms (指南者留学, 万能班长, myOffer). **CR5 is plausibly under 15%.** 小红书 has replaced Baidu SEM as the primary customer-acquisition battlefield, collapsing acquisition-cost advantages of scale and enabling boutique/IP-driven operators — but also flooding the market with unqualified sellers.
- **UK-side recruitment-agent economics**: UK universities pay agents commission (typically 10–15% of first-year tuition, i.e., £2,500–£5,000+ per enrolled student), which funds the "free agency" model common among UK-local Chinese agencies — a structural conflict of interest (agents steer to commission-paying, easier-admit universities) that a fee-for-service boutique can position against.

## 6. Pricing benchmarks by tier (价格带)

Market-observed 2024–2026 ranges for Chinese-market Master's application services:
- **Semi-DIY / document services** (指南者-type, 文书单项): **RMB 8,000–30,000**.
- **Mass-market full service, UK/HK/SG/AUS** (金吉列/新通/启德-type): **RMB 15,000–40,000** per country package; UK-local Chinese agencies often **free** (commission-funded) or nominal.
- **Mid-premium multi-country** (英港新联申 packages, stronger consultants): **RMB 40,000–80,000**.
- **Premium/US-centric boutiques** (世毕盟/再来人-type, mentor model): **RMB 60,000–150,000+**; top-school US graduate packages and PhD advisory **RMB 100,000–300,000**.
- **"保录取" (guaranteed admission) grey/black market**: RMB 300,000–1,000,000+ — periodically exposed in scandals (fabricated transcripts, proxy applicants); a live reputational and regulatory risk for the whole industry and a positioning foil for legitimate operators.
- Refund-linked ("不录取退款") structures are now table stakes in the premium tier.

## 7. Demand-side shifts (需求侧)

1. **考研分流 — the big one.** Registrations: 4.57M (2022) → **4.74M peak (2023)** → 4.38M (2024) → 3.88M (2025) → **~3.44M (2026)** — down ~1.3 million (−27%) in three years (教育部). Reasons: brutal odds (~4:1), 专硕 tuition inflation, and degraded ROI of domestic Master's in a weak job market. A visible fraction of quitters converts to overseas one-year Master's — agencies now run standard "考研后留学" (post-exam pivot) product lines every January–March, feeding HK/UK/AUS rounds that are still open. This is a structural, multi-year tailwind for exactly AcademiaOne's segment.
2. **就业导向 (employability-first).** With **12.22 million 2025 university graduates** (教育部) and urban youth (16–24, ex-students) unemployment hitting **18.9% (August 2025**, 国家统计局), the Master's has become a labour-market entry ticket. Choice logic is dominated by QS rankings because employers, 选调生 lists, and hukou policies key off them — e.g., **Shanghai grants direct hukou to graduates of QS Top-50 universities (Top 51–100: after 6 months' social insurance)**. This mechanically favours UK/HK/SG institutions that rank high per unit of admission difficulty and cost.
3. **性价比留学 (value-for-money).** RMB weakness against USD, middle-class balance-sheet stress (property), and consumption downgrade push families toward one-year degrees and near-shore destinations: total cost of a HK Master's (~RMB 300–500k all-in) vs a US Master's (RMB 1–1.5M+). Macau, Malaysia, and 中外合作办学 pick up the value tail.
4. **反向留学 discourse.** The viral complaint that UK/AUS one-year Master's classrooms are "全班中国人" — plus employer sniffing at "水硕" — has not dented volume but has shifted *product preference*: toward higher-ranked, harder programmes, STEM/quant conversion, and portfolios with internship/背景提升 components. It raises the value of honest selectivity advice — a boutique's natural terrain.
5. **Multi-destination hedging is now default.** The standard client buys UK+HK(+SG) simultaneously; US-only or UK-only mandates are shrinking. Agencies without genuine multi-market competence are being unbundled.

## 8. Regulation risk map

**China side:** (a) No re-licensing on the horizon, but the 保录取/materials-fraud scandals invite a future crackdown; (b) 广告法 exposure for guarantee/ranking claims; (c) PIPL data-security obligations for handling student personal data cross-border — relevant to a UK-headquartered firm serving mainland clients; (d) FX: the US$50,000/person/year individual quota (tuition remittances have documented channels but friction is real); (e) 双减 precedent — education is a politically exposed sector, though outbound consulting was untouched and even benefited (New Oriental's pivot); (f) rhetoric risk: state media periodically questions the value of foreign degrees; 平安留学 messaging on US safety.

**Destination side:** UK Graduate Route cut to 18 months + £925 levy from 2028 (margin pressure → tuition rises, but UK unis are financially dependent on Chinese PGT — one in four UK universities in deficit, giving China-recruitment a floor); US visa volatility (the single biggest swing factor — a hard Trump-era ban would reroute ~50–100k students/year mostly into UK/HK/SG, a *positive* shock for AcademiaOne's corridors); Australia's administrative caps; HK's opposite-direction risk — over-supply of TPG seats degrading brand value by the late 2020s, and mainland scrutiny of HK degrees' 含金量.

## 9. Outlook 2027–2030

- **Demographics support the Master's market through 2030.** The 18–22 cohort reflects 2005–2012 births (still 16M+/year); the birth collapse (17.86M in 2016 → 9.02M in 2023) hits undergraduate pipelines from ~2034 and Master's demand around 2038 — outside the planning window. 高考 registrations only just plateaued (13.42M in 2024 → 13.35M in 2025, first dip since 2017).
- **Base case**: outbound flow stable-to-growing at 700–800k/year; UK holds ~150k±10% Chinese stock (levy passed into fees, Graduate Route survives at 18 months); HK/SG/Macau continue gaining share, with HK TPG capacity possibly doubling by 2030; US grinds lower or stabilises ~250k depending on politics; AUS/CA range-bound by caps.
- **Industry structure**: continued fragmentation; AI compresses the low end (LLM-written 文书 destroys the document-mill business model) while raising the premium on judgment, school-side knowledge, verification, and accountability — the boutique thesis. Expect consolidation of demand around trusted IPs/brands on 小红书 rather than around big corporates; expect at least one 保录取 scandal-driven compliance wave; expect UK universities to tighten agent governance (agent quality framework), favouring registered, auditable partners.
- **Key watch-items for the founder**: Open Doors each November; HESA each spring (first data showing the 2025 US-refugee bump lands 2026–27); UK levy implementation detail; HK Policy Address quota moves; 考研 registrations each November (a leading indicator of the January–March pivot-season pipeline); RMB/GBP.

## 10. Implications for AcademiaOne

The data says AcademiaOne is positioned in the strongest corridor of the market (China → UK/HK/SG taught Master's) at the strongest moment of the cycle (考研 collapse + US rerouting + HK capacity build-out), inside an industry whose deregulated fragmentation punishes scale and rewards trust. The durable moats the numbers point to: verifiable outcome data (against an industry of unverifiable claims), fee-for-service independence (against commission-conflicted free agencies), genuine multi-destination selectivity expertise (as HK/SG admission bars inflate), and employability framing (QS/hukou/careers logic) — not influencer volume.

结构上，硕士是绝对主力：赴英中国学生约六成为研究生（约9万人，以一年制授课硕士为主），白皮书调研显示七至八成留学意向指向硕博。需求侧三大变量利好：考研报名人数从2023年474万峰值三连降至2026年约344万（-27%），"考研后转留学"成为标准产品线；2025届高校毕业生1222万、8月份16-24岁青年失业率18.9%，就业导向使QS排名与落户政策（如上海QS前50直接落户）主导择校；人民币走弱与消费降级推动"性价比留学"，利好港新英一年制。

行业侧：2017年国务院取消留学中介资质认定后行业彻底碎片化，相关企业超8万家，CR5估计不足15%；狭义中介市场约300–500亿元人民币（艾瑞/沙利文口径估算）。价格带分层清晰：半DIY八千至三万、大众全案1.5–4万、中高端联申4–8万、高端美式精品6–15万+，"保录取"灰产30万以上且丑闻频发。对AcademiaOne的启示：公司正处于最强赛道（中国→英港新硕士）与最佳周期（考研分流+美国分流+香港扩容）的交汇点；护城河应建立在可验证的录取结果数据、收费制独立顾问定位（对冲返佣式免费中介的利益冲突）、多目的地真实选校能力与就业导向叙事之上，而非流量网红打法。2030年前硕士适龄人口无忧，人口悬崖影响在2035年后才传导至本市场。
